A Digital Twin Framework for Enhanced Rock weathering : Towards a robust decision support system and MRV strategies.
The code includes components for an end-to-end virtual modeling for an Enhanced Rock weathering project, starting with a basic assessment of the Enhanced Rock weathering potential and mineral reactivity based on geochemical datasets (from repositories like PetDB and Georoc and using methods like CIPW norm to get nominal mineralogy) of a location, an assessment of the soil composition and pH (especially the spatial variability and structure) using public point datasets (like soil chemistry measurements) and regional-global spatial datasets such as iSDA and soildb (30 m resolution using machine learning and geospatial datasets). Then, we can do use these maps as inputs, or synthetic texture maps for fractal noise, variograms, and neutral landscape models, to model different strategies for soil sampling/mixing. We use the spatial maps as the spatial patterns, choose the values of variance of each individual element like Ti, Ca, Mg, .. as well as the covariance (using a chosen covariance matrix), and then make synthetic soil samples using multi-component and multi-element mixing models with soil, rock, and weathered end-members. These synthetic datasets, with realistic variability, is then analyzed in a similar approach to the real world data to assess the weathering fraction, as well as the how uncertainity in the estimate changes as a function of number of samples analyzed. Our general framework allows us to directly incorporate measurement errors, differences in sampling strategies (pooling, grinding, coarse vs fine fraction dominance) and different spatial variability in the simulations. All of these are combined together to assess overall cost curve analysis with regards to optimizing carbon sequesteration given a total investment, managing risks (due to uncertainity from inversion and associated carbon credit income as well as risk from harmful element enrichment in the soil like Cr, Ni), and ensuring that the whole operation is net profitable. We will present results from different model scenarios showing the tradeoff between the number of samples (and associated costs) vs measurement accuracies (higher vs lower accuracy methods like ICP-MS vs LIBS/XRF, each have different costs and startup investments, as well as measurement centralization vs not which adds financial risk) for different underlying spatial heterogenieties and different amount of rock deployment. These results illustrate some optimal deployment and MRV strategies as well as decision guidelines for optimizing ERW success in different locations globally. We also include a set of simplified soil reactive transport models to assess how the soil pH and alkanity evolves over time incorporating some effects of soil organic carbon decomposition, soil degassing, carbonate ion speciation, and change in rainfall and seasonal temperature changes. 

The focus of our framework is to have a hierarchy of computationally efficient models that can then feed frameworks for  multi-objective optimization strategies and help make robust decision support with regards to  where to depploy ERW, what strategies to focus on, and the optimal tradeoff of different strategies and costs associated with MRV. Our results demonstrate that there can be some non-standard optimal strategies for ERW that focus not on resolving the exact amount of weathered material with a large number of samples, but instead of determining A lower bound signal of weathering above the noise level but with a much lower number of correctly sampled soil samples.

Enhanced Rock Weathering (ERW) is a promising, globally scalable, and potentially economically feasible carbon dioxide removal strategy with co-benefits for soil health. However, scaling ERW to Megaton-Gigaton scale requires robust methods to quantify carbon sequestration accurately (MRV), manage deployment risks, and ensure economic viability amid significant soil spatial heterogeneity and measurement uncertainties. 

We present a novel digital twin framework designed as an end-to-end decision support system for ERW projects designed specifically to address these challenges. It integrates geochemical weathering and reactivity potential assessment - utilizing public geochemical databases like PetDB, and mineralogy estimates using CIPW norm -  with high-resolution (30m) spatial soil characterization via either datasets like iSDA/soildb or synthetic maps generated using fractal noise/variograms. We then use multi-element, multi-end member mixing models to simulate realistic soil-rock mixtures, explicitly incorporating spatial variability, measurement errors (ICP-MS vs. LIBS/XRF), and diverse sampling strategies (pooling, grinding). In parallel, we use simplified reactive transport models to calculate soil pH/alkalinity evolution under climate dynamics based on the dynamic, ecohydrological, and biogeochemical Soil Model for Enhanced Weathering (SMEW). Finally, we use the synthetic "datasets" to invert for the amount of feedstock weathering and the associated uncertainities as a function of number of samples. Overall, our new framework help us quantify sequestration efficiency and uncertainty as a function of sampling density and analytical precision. This in turn enables integrated cost-curve analysis and risk assessment, optimizing carbon sequestration against investment costs, measurement uncertainty (impacting credit income), and potential toxic element enrichment (e.g., Cr, Ni).

We will use results using this framework to illustrate tradeoff between the number of samples, sampling strategies, measurement uncertainity, and the nature of spatial soil heterogeneity using both soil tracer approach (like TiCat) and pH/alkalinity sensors. Our results also demonstrate the potential of some non-standard optimal strategies for ERW: Identifying a statistically robust lower bound for weathering signals above noise levels, frequently achievable with signficantly fewer samples than traditional high-precision quantification, and thus significantly reduces MRV costs. The hierarchy of computationally efficient models in our Digital Twin framework facilitates multi-objective optimization, providing prescriptive guidance on optimal deployment locations, sampling/analytical methods, and MRV cost-accuracy-risk trade-offs for global scalability and profitability.
